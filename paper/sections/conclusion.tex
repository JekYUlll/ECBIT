\section{Conclusion}

This study shows that sparse Antarctic AWS imputation benefits most from matching the missingness curriculum to realistic contiguous outages and conditioning on physically coherent ERA5 background fields. ERA5-conditioned models consistently outperform station-only models under block failures, and the advantage increases with outage length. Station-season calibrated ERA5 is itself a strong operational baseline; ERA5-augmented neural models provide a modest additional gain by combining ERA5 trajectories with sparse AWS temporal and cross-variable context. Fair augmented baselines show similar performance for gated ECBIT, concat ECBIT, and iTransformer+ERA5, while SAITS+ERA5 obtains the lowest aggregate MAE but is statistically tied with gated ECBIT in paired tests. Held-out station results further show that Antarctic station generalization is heterogeneous and should be reported explicitly rather than collapsed into a single average. Future work should focus on station-adaptive ERA5 bias correction, uncertainty-aware imputation, realistic-gap surrogate evaluation, and broader polar AWS variables.
