\section{Conclusion}

This study shows that for sparse Antarctic AWS imputation, matching the missingness curriculum to realistic contiguous outages and conditioning on physically coherent ERA5 background fields are more important than introducing a specialized fusion layer. ERA5-conditioned models consistently outperform station-only models under block failures, and the advantage increases with outage length. Fair augmented baselines indicate that the main contribution is the benchmark and empirical finding rather than architecture dominance: gated ECBIT, concat ECBIT, and iTransformer+ERA5 perform similarly, while SAITS+ERA5 obtains the lowest aggregate MAE but is statistically tied with gated ECBIT in paired tests. Held-out station results further show that Antarctic station generalization is heterogeneous and should be reported explicitly rather than collapsed into a single average. Future work should focus on station-adaptive ERA5 bias correction, uncertainty-aware imputation, realistic-gap surrogate evaluation, and broader polar AWS variables.
