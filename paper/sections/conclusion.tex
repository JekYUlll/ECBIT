\section{Conclusions}

This paper proposed a reanalysis-conditioned macro--micro coupled framework for block-missing imputation of sparse Antarctic AWS records. The framework treats ERA5 as a macro-scale physical background and AWS observations as the micro-scale label and constraint layer, then evaluates restoration under realistic contiguous outage masks. Matching the training mask to the block-failure mode and conditioning on ERA5 are the two factors that most improve recovery: ERA5-conditioned block-trained models reduce normalized MAE from 0.346 to 0.258 relative to block-trained no-ERA5 models, and the advantage grows monotonically with outage length. Station-season calibrated ERA5 direct substitution (0.270 MAE) is already a strong operational macro-background baseline; ERA5-augmented neural models add a modest but statistically significant increment by integrating sparse AWS temporal and cross-variable context. Once ERA5 is aligned and the block-missing curriculum matches the operational failure mode, several architectures achieve similar accuracy: gated ECBIT, concat ECBIT, and iTransformer+ERA5 are statistically indistinguishable, while SAITS+ERA5 achieves the lowest aggregate mean without a significant paired advantage. Held-out MAE ranges from 0.173 (Nico) to 0.350 (Mount Sidley), confirming that station-level results must be reported rather than pooled. Priority directions for follow-on work include station-adaptive ERA5 bias correction, probabilistic imputation under block failures, and extension to radiation and precipitation variables.
