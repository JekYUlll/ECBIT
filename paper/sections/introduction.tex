\section{Introduction}

Continuous near-surface meteorological observations are central to Antarctic climate analysis, model evaluation, and field operations. Automatic weather stations (AWSs) extend coverage beyond staffed stations, but the same remoteness that makes them valuable also makes their records incomplete. The AntAWS compilation aggregates Antarctic AWS observations at 3-hourly, daily, and monthly resolutions and documents the role of AWS networks in resolving remote Antarctic conditions \citep{wang2023antaws}. In the selected 3-hourly stations used in this work, missingness is not well described by independent random points: wind speed and relative humidity contain long contiguous gaps, and several stations exhibit outage blocks lasting days to weeks.

The task is imputation, not forecasting: the model reconstructs missing station observations within a window while leaving available observations unchanged. ERA5 reanalysis provides a physically coherent meteorological background \citep{hersbach2020era5}, but using it naively can be counterproductive: when AWS observations are present, ERA5 often duplicates already available temperature and pressure information; when relative humidity is biased, always-on fusion can inject error. We therefore use ERA5 as a conditional prior that is activated by missingness.

\subsection{Macro--Micro Coupled Restoration}
Antarctic AWS imputation is not a standard time-series interpolation problem. It is a conditional restoration problem in which two physically distinct information layers must be coupled: a dense macro-scale reanalysis background and a sparse micro-scale in situ record. Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}$ denote a station-level AWS window with $T=168$ 3-hourly steps and $C=5$ variables, let $E \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}$ denote the aligned ERA5 sequence at the same timestamps and variable ordering, and let $O \in \{0,1\}^{T \times C}$ denote the real observation availability mask. With temporal structure $\tau$ and station location or metadata $s_i$, the general restoration operator is
\begin{equation}
\hat{X} = \mathcal{R}(\tilde{X}, O, E, \tau; s_i), \quad \tilde{X}=X\odot O.
\label{eq:mmcr}
\end{equation}
The key property of Eq.~\ref{eq:mmcr} is that $E$ enters as a structured prior rather than as a target: station observations remain the reconstruction target whenever they are available, and the reanalysis background is most relevant when station evidence is absent.

This framing unifies four components of the study. The macro background extraction layer recovers a station-location ERA5 series from the gridded reanalysis field. The micro observation masking layer represents the station record through its real availability mask and the block-failure process caused by icing, power interruptions, and maintenance gaps. The macro--micro bias calibration layer acknowledges that ERA5 is not ground truth; station observations can be viewed as a reanalysis background plus station-, month-, and variable-specific bias and local residual. The conditional restoration layer instantiates $\mathcal{R}$ as a learned model with a copy-back rule that preserves values visible in the input context.

The proposed framework uses ECBIT, an ERA5-conditioned block imputation transformer, as a lightweight gated instantiation of this restoration operator. One stream encodes normalized AWS values, observation masks, and time features; the other encodes aligned ERA5 variables. A conditioning module projects ERA5 tokens into the AWS embedding space, and a missing-variable gate restricts ERA5 influence to channels that contain missing positions. Loss is computed only on artificially hidden positions that were genuinely observed in the raw record, so the benchmark and label support remain fixed while ERA5 access, fusion form, and mask curriculum are varied independently. SAITS+ERA5 and iTransformer+ERA5 are included as aligned-covariate instantiations to test whether the main effect comes from reanalysis access rather than the specific ECBIT fusion block.

The current benchmark contains 32 selected AntAWS stations with at least 10 years of record and sufficient multi-variable completeness. Five geographically diverse stations are reserved for held-out generalization. ERA5 point time series are downloaded and aligned to the exact AntAWS 3-hourly timestamps, producing 41,388 sparse 168-step windows. The experimental matrix covers interpolation, last-observation-carried-forward, ERA5 direct substitution, SAITS \citep{du2023saits}, an iTransformer-style imputation baseline \citep{liu2024itransformer}, ERA5-augmented SAITS/iTransformer controls, and ECBIT ablations.

This study proposes a reanalysis-conditioned macro--micro coupled framework for block-missing imputation of Antarctic automatic weather station records. The framework formalizes sparse AWS recovery as a conditional restoration problem, instantiates it with lightweight ERA5-conditioned neural models, and validates it through controlled block-missing protocols. Its central empirical result is that ERA5 conditioning and failure-mode-matched block masking are the main measured factors in recovering prolonged Antarctic AWS outages. Once ERA5 is aligned and the block-missing curriculum matches the operational failure mode, several architectures achieve similar accuracy.

The paper reports this contribution through three elements:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a problem formulation that casts Antarctic AWS block-missing imputation as macro--micro coupled restoration, linking sparse station observations, dense reanalysis background, real observation masks, temporal structure, and station-specific ERA5 bias within Eq.~\ref{eq:mmcr};
  \item a framework instantiation and evaluation protocol that align selected 3-hourly AntAWS station windows with ERA5 point series, sparse observation masks, block-missing simulations, held-out station splits, station-month ERA5 bias calibration, and ERA5-conditioned neural variants;
  \item empirical evidence that ERA5 conditioning reduces block-trained MAE from 0.346 to 0.258, MCAR-trained ERA5 models transfer poorly to block tests (0.407 MAE), and matched ERA5-augmented controls with SAITS and iTransformer place the main effect on reanalysis access and failure-mode matching rather than a particular fusion block.
\end{itemize}
