\section{Introduction}

Continuous near-surface meteorological observations are central to Antarctic climate analysis, model evaluation, and field operations. Automatic weather stations (AWSs) extend coverage beyond staffed stations, but the same remoteness that makes them valuable also makes their records incomplete. The AntAWS compilation aggregates Antarctic AWS observations at 3-hourly, daily, and monthly resolutions and documents the role of AWS networks in resolving remote Antarctic conditions \citep{wang2023antaws}. In the selected 3-hourly stations used in this work, missingness is not well described by independent random points: wind speed and relative humidity contain long contiguous gaps, and several stations exhibit outage blocks lasting days to weeks.

The model reconstructs missing station observations within each window while leaving available AWS values unchanged. ERA5 reanalysis provides a physically coherent meteorological background \citep{hersbach2020era5}, but using it naively can be counterproductive: when AWS observations are present, ERA5 often duplicates already available temperature and pressure information; when relative humidity is biased, always-on fusion can inject error. We therefore use ERA5 as a conditional prior that is activated by missingness.

\subsection{Reanalysis-Conditioned Restoration Formulation}
In the selected AWS windows, missing intervals often last for days, while ERA5 supplies a continuous meteorological trajectory over the same period. Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}$ denote a station-level AWS window with $T=168$ 3-hourly steps and $C=5$ variables, let $E \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}$ denote the aligned ERA5 sequence at the same timestamps and variable ordering, and let $O \in \{0,1\}^{T \times C}$ denote the real observation availability mask. With temporal structure $\tau$ and station location or metadata $s_i$, the restoration operator is
\begin{equation}
\hat{X} = \mathcal{R}(\tilde{X}, O, E, \tau; s_i), \quad \tilde{X}=X\odot O.
\label{eq:mmcr}
\end{equation}
In Eq.~\ref{eq:mmcr}, $E$ is not a target. Station observations remain the label source whenever they are available, and ERA5 is used to support reconstruction when station evidence is absent.

In practice, the implementation follows the data flow. ERA5 is extracted at each station location and aligned to the AntAWS 3-hourly timestamps. The raw AWS record remains sparse through its observation mask; artificial labels are sampled only from originally observed positions, and pre-existing gaps are excluded from the loss. ERA5-AWS bias is handled either explicitly, through train-only station-month calibration, or implicitly, through a learned restoration model. The final imputed series copies visible AWS values back after decoding.

ECBIT is the gated model used for the main ablation. One stream encodes normalized AWS values, observation masks, and time features; the other encodes aligned ERA5 variables. A conditioning module projects ERA5 tokens into the AWS embedding space, and a missing-variable gate restricts ERA5 influence to channels that contain missing positions. Loss is computed only on artificially hidden positions that were genuinely observed in the raw record, so the benchmark and label support remain fixed while ERA5 access, fusion form, and mask curriculum are varied independently. SAITS+ERA5 and iTransformer+ERA5 use aligned ERA5 covariates as controls, allowing the effect of ERA5 access to be checked without tying the conclusion to the ECBIT gate.

The current benchmark contains 32 selected AntAWS stations with at least 10 years of record and sufficient multi-variable completeness. Five geographically diverse stations are reserved for held-out generalization. ERA5 point time series are downloaded and aligned to the exact AntAWS 3-hourly timestamps, producing 41,388 sparse 168-step windows. The experimental matrix covers interpolation, last-observation-carried-forward, ERA5 direct substitution, SAITS \citep{du2023saits}, an iTransformer-style imputation baseline \citep{liu2024itransformer}, ERA5-augmented SAITS/iTransformer controls, and ECBIT ablations.

We use this setup to organize the benchmark and experiments. The study first aligns sparse AWS windows with ERA5 point series, then tests whether ERA5 access and block-missing training improve recovery under simulated outages. The main result is that ERA5 conditioning and failure-mode-matched block masking drive most of the measured improvement. Once ERA5 is aligned and the block-missing curriculum matches the operational failure mode, several architectures achieve similar accuracy.

The paper contributes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a station-level restoration formulation that links sparse AWS observations, aligned ERA5 background, real observation masks, temporal structure, and station-specific ERA5 bias within Eq.~\ref{eq:mmcr};
  \item an AntAWS-derived benchmark and evaluation protocol with 3-hourly station windows, ERA5 point series, sparse observation masks, block-missing simulations, held-out stations, station-month ERA5 calibration, and ERA5-conditioned neural variants;
  \item empirical evidence that ERA5 conditioning reduces block-trained MAE from 0.346 to 0.258, MCAR-trained ERA5 models transfer poorly to block tests (0.407 MAE), and matched ERA5-augmented controls with SAITS and iTransformer place the main effect on reanalysis access and failure-mode matching rather than a particular fusion block.
\end{itemize}
