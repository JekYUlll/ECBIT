\subsection{Polar AWS Records and Reanalysis Products}
The AntAWS dataset compiles quality-controlled AWS observations across Antarctica and provides 3-hourly meteorological variables including air temperature, pressure, relative humidity, and wind \citep{wang2023antaws}. These records include gaps associated with winter darkness, icing, snow burial, power interruptions, and infrequent maintenance access. The IMAU Antarctic AWS archive adds a complementary long-term Antarctic record with additional surface-energy and surface-height variables at 19 locations from 1995--2022 \citep{vantiggelen2025imau}.

Reanalysis products provide spatially complete, physically constrained background fields that are attractive for sparse polar settings. ERA5 provides hourly global atmospheric variables and is widely used as a modern reanalysis baseline \citep{hersbach2020era5}. However, Antarctic validation studies show that reanalysis should not be treated as ground truth. ERA5 near-surface temperature over Antarctica has high monthly correlation with station observations but exhibits regionally structured biases \citep{zhu2021era5antarctica}. In the Antarctic Peninsula--Ellsworth Land region, ERA5 performance depends on parameter and geographic context \citep{tetzner2019era5antarcticpeninsula}. Coastal and continent-wide wind evaluations likewise show that ERA5 is useful for Antarctic near-surface winds while retaining terrain- and regime-dependent biases near complex orography and coastal margins \citep{catonharrison2022antarcticwinds,rakoczy2026era5winds}. In the experiments below, ERA5 is used as background information for masked intervals, while available AWS measurements are kept in the output.

Recent reanalysis-informed reconstruction studies have mainly focused on gridded fields. \citet{ma2025antarcticsat} train a deep model that combines reanalysis inputs and observation masks to reconstruct monthly Antarctic surface air temperature from sparse in situ records. \citet{li2026era5superresolution} study a related ERA5-processing problem, showing that a ResNet-based super-resolution model can transfer across regions and variables. Both studies operate on gridded spatial fields. Here, the task is station-level AWS time-series imputation within sparse windows, with observed values preserved and ERA5 used as aligned background information for masked entries.

\subsection{Time-Series Imputation}
Classical imputation baselines such as linear interpolation and carry-forward filling are strong sanity checks for short gaps but do not model cross-variable structure. Non-parametric methods such as MissForest can exploit cross-sectional feature relations but are not designed around ordered temporal dynamics \citep{stekhoven2012missforest}. Deep imputation methods add temporal modeling and learned latent structure. GRU-D incorporates missingness indicators and time gaps into recurrent modeling \citep{che2018grud}. BRITS learns missing values inside a bidirectional recurrent computation graph \citep{cao2018brits}. SAITS uses self-attention and missingness-aware weighting for multivariate time-series imputation \citep{du2023saits}. GP-VAE and CSDI frame imputation probabilistically with latent variables or score-based generative models \citep{fortuin2020gpvae,tashiro2021csdi}.

Imputation performance is tightly coupled to the assumed missingness mechanism. \citet{fang2020imputationsurvey} survey deep imputation architectures and their treatment of temporal structure; \citet{wang2024imputationsurvey} organize multivariate methods by uncertainty modeling and neural architecture; and TSI-Bench standardizes evaluation across algorithms, datasets, and several missingness scenarios \citep{du2024tsibench}. TSI-Bench asks how generic algorithms rank under benchmark protocols; the present study asks whether a domain-specific block-missing curriculum and ERA5 conditioning improve Antarctic AWS recovery under realistic contiguous outages.

\subsection{Transformers for Time Series}
The gated neural model uses transformer encoders because self-attention provides a flexible mechanism for variable-level interaction and long-window context modeling \citep{vaswani2017attention}. Time-series transformer designs have also departed from direct language-model tokenization. Informer and Autoformer introduced efficient attention and decomposition ideas for long-sequence forecasting \citep{zhou2021informer,wu2021autoformer}. FEDformer combines decomposition and frequency-domain attention for long-horizon forecasting \citep{zhou2022fedformer}. PatchTST segments time series into patch tokens and uses channel-independent sharing for efficient long-context modeling \citep{nie2023patchtst}. TimesNet maps one-dimensional temporal variation into two-dimensional periodic structures and reports broad performance across forecasting, imputation, classification, and anomaly detection \citep{wu2023timesnet}. iTransformer inverts the standard representation and treats each variate as a token, allowing attention to operate over variables rather than individual timestamps \citep{liu2024itransformer}.

For AWS records, variable tokens keep each meteorological channel's 168-step trajectory intact and place the attention matrix over channels. ECBIT uses this iTransformer-style representation for masked reconstruction in sparse AWS windows. Each token is built from one variable's AWS trajectory, observation mask, and time features; the five tokens correspond to temperature, pressure, wind, relative humidity, and specific humidity.

\subsection{Exogenous Conditioning and Domain-Specific Restoration}
Recent auxiliary-fusion studies warn against unconstrained use of exogenous information. \citet{lee2026constrainedfusion} show that naive multimodal fusion degrades performance when auxiliary modalities carry irrelevant information, and propose the Controlled Fusion Adapter (CFA) to constrain the auxiliary contribution. \citet{cao2026ecto} argue for physically grounded variable selection rather than treating all meteorological channels as equally informative. \citet{chen2025exost} document inconsistent exogenous effects and address them with the ExoST ``select, then balance'' framework.

The closest domain analogue is ST-DAN, which combines a transformer temporal path with a graph-attention path for buoy meteorological data restoration, with ERA5 reanalysis used as a training reference and in situ buoy observations used for testing \citep{song2026stdan}. The present benchmark instead focuses on Antarctic AWS contiguous outages and compares block-trained with MCAR-trained models under matched block tests. The ablation results then show that a lightweight conditioning mechanism is sufficient once ERA5 alignment and block-curriculum matching are correct.
