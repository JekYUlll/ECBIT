\title{Block-Missing Imputation of Antarctic Automatic Weather Station Records with ERA5 Reanalysis Conditioning}

\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Antarctic automatic weather stations provide essential in situ meteorological observations, but their time series are often sparse because of sensor icing, power interruptions, and winter maintenance gaps. We study block-wise imputation for 3-hourly Antarctic station records using ERA5 as an external physical prior. We construct an AntAWS-derived benchmark with 32 selected stations, aligned ERA5 time series, realistic block-missing simulation, and held-out station splits, and use a lightweight ERA5-conditioned variate-token transformer as a controlled architecture for testing when reanalysis information is useful. Under matched block-missing training, ERA5 conditioning reduces overall MAE from 0.346 to 0.258. Fair ERA5-augmented SAITS and iTransformer baselines show that the gain is mainly attributable to ERA5 access and block-missing curriculum matching rather than to a unique gated-fusion advantage: SAITS+ERA5 reaches 0.245 MAE, while iTransformer+ERA5, gated ECBIT, and concat ECBIT all cluster near 0.258. Models trained with point-wise MCAR masks transfer poorly to block tests, with MAE 0.407 even when ERA5 is available. Follow-up analyses show that the ERA5 benefit grows from 13.8\% for 24-hour blocks to 26.4\% for 216-hour blocks, is concentrated in temperature, wind speed, and specific-humidity channels, and remains stable when ERA5 is downsampled to 6-hour resolution.
\end{abstract}
