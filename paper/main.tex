\title{Reanalysis-Conditioned Block-Missing Imputation for Antarctic Automatic Weather Station Records}

\author[seu]{Yongzhe Li\corref{cor1}}
\ead{yongzheli@seu.edu.cn}
\author[seu]{Zhuyu Zhang}
\ead{220245154@seu.edu.cn}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\address[seu]{School of Mechanical Engineering, Southeast University, Nanjing, China}

\begin{abstract}
Sensor icing, power failures, and infrequent maintenance cause Antarctic automatic weather station (AWS) records to fail in contiguous multi-day blocks. We treat Antarctic AWS imputation as a station-level restoration problem constrained by sparse in situ observations and supported by ERA5 reanalysis as a dense but biased meteorological background. Using an AntAWS-derived benchmark of 32 selected 3-hourly stations, we align ERA5 point time series with sparse station windows, simulate block-missing outages, reserve held-out stations, and compare calibrated ERA5 substitution with lightweight ERA5-conditioned neural models. When training masks match the block-failure regime, ERA5 conditioning reduces normalized MAE from 0.346 to 0.258; the relative gain grows from 13.8\% at 24 h to 26.4\% at 216 h. Models trained with point-wise MCAR masks transfer poorly to block tests (MAE 0.407 with ERA5), indicating that failure-mode matching is essential. Fair ERA5-augmented comparisons with SAITS and iTransformer show that aligned reanalysis access and block-missing curriculum matching explain the main empirical gains, while several fusion parameterizations are statistically similar. Prolonged Antarctic AWS outages are therefore more reliably recovered when reanalysis conditioning is paired with failure-mode-matched block training.
\end{abstract}
