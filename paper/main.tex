\title{Block-missing imputation of Antarctic automatic weather station records using ERA5 reanalysis conditioning}

\author[seu]{Yongzhe Li\corref{cor1}}
\ead{yongzheli@seu.edu.cn}
\author[seu]{Zhuyu Zhang}
\ead{220245154@seu.edu.cn}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\address[seu]{School of Mechanical Engineering, Southeast University, Nanjing, China}

\begin{abstract}
Sensor icing, power failures, and infrequent maintenance cause Antarctic automatic weather station (AWS) records to fail in contiguous multi-day blocks rather than as isolated missing points. This study evaluates block-wise imputation of 3-hourly Antarctic AWS observations using ERA5 reanalysis as an external physical prior. We construct an AntAWS-derived benchmark containing 32 selected stations, aligned ERA5 point time series, sparse observation masks, realistic block-missing simulations, and held-out station splits. When training masks match the block-failure regime, ERA5 conditioning reduces normalized MAE from 0.346 to 0.258; the relative gain grows monotonically from 13.8\% at 24 h to 26.4\% at 216 h. Fair ERA5-augmented comparisons with SAITS and iTransformer show that the main gain comes from ERA5 access and block-missing curriculum matching rather than from a particular fusion block. Models trained with point-wise MCAR masks transfer poorly to block tests, indicating that failure-mode matching is essential for Antarctic AWS imputation. The results demonstrate that reanalysis-conditioned imputation can improve recovery of sparse polar station records during prolonged outages.
\end{abstract}
