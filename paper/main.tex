\title{ERA5-Conditioned Block Imputation for Sparse Antarctic Weather Station Time Series}

\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Antarctic automatic weather stations provide essential in situ meteorological observations, but their time series are often sparse because of sensor icing, power interruptions, and winter maintenance gaps. We study block-wise imputation for 3-hourly Antarctic station records using ERA5 as an external physical prior. We construct an AntAWS-derived benchmark with 32 selected stations, aligned ERA5 time series, realistic block-missing simulation, and held-out station splits, and use a lightweight ERA5-conditioned variate-token transformer to test when reanalysis information is useful. A completed factorial ablation shows that block-missing curriculum and ERA5 conditioning are complementary: block curriculum contributes an average 0.166 MAE reduction, ERA5 contributes 0.105, and their combination gives the best overall MAE of 0.258. Gated and concat fusion perform similarly, indicating that the information source and missingness regime matter more than a specific fusion layer. Follow-up analyses show that the ERA5 benefit grows from 13.8\% for 24-hour blocks to 26.4\% for 216-hour blocks, is concentrated in temperature, wind speed, and humidity channels, and remains stable when ERA5 is downsampled to 6-hour resolution.
\end{abstract}
